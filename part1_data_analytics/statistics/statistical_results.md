# Part 1 – Statistical Analysis Results

Generated by `statistics/statistical_analysis.py` from `sql/metro_traffic.db` (raw data, 48,204 rows).
Sections 1–3 reproduce the SQL analyses in `sql/part1_queries.sql`; sections 4–5 add formal hypothesis tests.

## 1. Descriptive statistics – traffic volume

Mean 3,260 · median 3,380 · std 1,987 · min 0 · max 7,280 · skewness -0.09. The distribution is nearly symmetric overall but multi-modal (quiet nights vs busy daytime hours), so the mean alone is a poor summary.

## 2. Correlation – temperature vs traffic volume

| Measure | Value | p-value |
|---|---|---|
| Pearson r (raw, as in SQL) | 0.1303 | 1.64e-181 |
| Pearson r (0 K faults removed) | 0.1323 | 4.71e-187 |
| Spearman ρ (cleaned) | 0.1326 | 6.80e-188 |

A **weak positive** relationship: statistically significant because n is large, but temperature explains only 1.8% of the variance in volume. Correlation is not causation; time of day and season drive both variables.

## 3. Probability analysis – congestion (> 5,500 vehicles/hour) and weather

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| P(Congestion) | 0.1473 |
| P(Clear) | 0.2778 |
| P(Congestion ∩ Clear) | 0.0366 |
| P(Congestion) × P(Clear) | 0.0409 |
| P(Clear \| Congestion) | 0.2483 |
| P(High temp > 292 K \| Congestion) | 0.2630 |
| Odds of congestion, clear | 0.1516 |
| Odds of congestion, clouds | 0.2062 |
| **Odds ratio clear vs clouds** | **0.7354** |

The joint probability is close to the product of the marginals, so congestion and clear weather are roughly independent. The odds of congestion are about 26% lower in clear than in cloudy weather.

## 4. Chi-square test of independence – weather × congestion

χ² = 165.8, df = 9, p = 4.65e-31, **Cramér's V = 0.059**.

The association is statistically significant, but its strength is very small (V < 0.1). Weather is not a practically important driver of congestion on its own.

## 5. Welch t-test – weekday vs weekend hourly volume

Weekday mean 3,534 vs weekend mean 2,571; t = 55.7, p < 1e-300, **Cohen's d = 0.53**.

Weekdays carry significantly more traffic, with a medium effect size. Pooling all hours dilutes the much larger peak-hour difference, which the Power BI dashboard shows directly.
